Live Board classroom flow — 12 November 2026

Insert live board opens the activity library, followed by a setup screen for the
title, instructions, visual layout, background, replies and reactions. The board
is created as a teacher draft. Supported shared layouts are Wall, Grid, Rows and
Timeline; structured activities also offer Columns and their graphic organizer.
Ranking activities retain their individual student columns.

Open to class publishes one shared board and places a reference page in each
enrolled student's notebook, in the matching section of the teacher notebook.
Custom sections are created if needed. A standalone board without a notebook
placement or lesson link goes into Notes. Teacher notes, files and assessments
on the source page are not copied. Existing student writing is never replaced.

Already-distributed references count as placed. Deterministic page IDs prevent
duplicates during retries or reopening. Deleted copies are not resurrected.
Protected sections are respected; a separate shared section is used if needed.
Students joining later receive published activities when their notebook opens
or polls. Legacy published boards gain placements when a teacher opens them.
Staff planning boards remain staff-only and do not create student pages.

The live classroom canvas is shared by teacher and student. Teacher controls
are in a cog drawer: posting state, appearance, layouts, teams, standards,
assessment, snapshots, archival and a retryable Sync student notebooks action.
Per-post moderation actions are in an ellipsis menu. Students cannot change
board settings or distribute boards. Existing class authorization remains in
force for board data, images, snapshots and contributions.

Notebook page, section and storage limits still apply. A partial delivery is
reported in the settings drawer and may be retried. Board delivery failures
do not prevent students opening or editing their other notebook work.

Validation: automated placement, retry, legacy migration, late enrolment,
permissions, private-content isolation, layout persistence and existing board
tests; fictional teacher/student browser walkthrough; desktop, tablet and
dark-mode visual checks. No actual classroom records were changed for testing.

Four flagship templates — implementation update

The library now prioritizes Venn Diagram, Our Reading Wall, Cause & Effect,
and Problem–Solution. Existing advanced templates retain their section data.

Venn Diagram uses two overlapping circles. Contributions are stored once in
left/shared/right regions; each region scrolls independently. A post can be
moved with drag and drop or its Move to selector. The latter is available on
touch and keyboard devices. Teachers may rename all three headings through
Activity & standards; headings are saved with the board and its snapshots.
The explicit List view remains available. Responses expand in a readable dialog.

Our Reading Wall has nine response types: question, connection, evidence worth
revisiting, prediction, inference, character thoughts/feelings/motivations,
theme/message, author choices, and changed thinking. Add post starts with a
response chooser. The editable subject is prefilled from the chosen type. The
main response and optional Evidence are the only default writing areas. Sources
belong in Evidence; Extensions & extras reveals the optional reasoning prompt. Typed evidence survives edits,
draft recovery, shared reads and saved snapshots. Text, uploaded images and links
use the existing authorization and upload checks. No camera/microphone capture
was introduced.

Cause & Effect and Problem–Solution keep each pair together in one contribution.
Both sides are required. Evidence/source and the explanation remain attached.
Each has its own layout with connected paper notes. The board title and both
column headings remain pinned inside the scrolling response area.

Ten independently generated quiet paper/material/painted backgrounds are
available. Texturelabs originals are not included or used as AI inputs.
Original stationery pieces are extracted from the generated sheet for paper,
sticky-note and taped-note surfaces. Generated hand-drawn arrows connect paired
responses. Nine-slice paper edges preserve their texture as posts grow. Paired notes stay
within a 360-pixel column, with negative space on larger displays. Prediction
paper is mirrored so its clip sits on the right. Reading Wall stationery is
assigned by response type; names and avatars continue to identify contributors. Live Boards use their own paper appearance, independent of the site theme. Full sheets and seven design mockups remain
in the separate design-round folder.

Validation uses fictional students, isolated local browser sessions and in-memory
API fixtures. No classroom records were edited. Production deployment is separate.

The Reading Wall header groups the title, class state and response filter. Generic
response-choice instructions are hidden; custom teacher instructions remain visible.
Fullscreen and View in new window are available inside embedded and standalone
boards. Fullscreen keeps composing and expanded responses available; new windows
retain board/snapshot identity and open with no reference to the original window.

Teachers can choose built-in images/patterns or upload a background from the
settings cog (PNG, JPEG, WebP or GIF, up to 5 MB). Uploads use the board’s existing
authenticated image storage; an image cannot be selected from another board.
Students see the selected image only while they can access the board. Snapshots
retain their background reference. Unused uploads are not exposed to students.
Poppins Bold headings and Sofia Sans Condensed body text are self-hosted defaults.
The separate view requests a resizable standard browser window. The operating
system/browser owns the green macOS window control; no webpage can enable it.

Background images fill the board as one centered image using cover sizing,
without tiling. They stay fixed within the board viewport while posts scroll.

Story Mountain uses five stable plot-stage columns: Exposition, Rising action,
Climax, Falling action, and Resolution. A losslessly cropped version of the
original generated landscape centers the summit under the third (Climax) column.
The landscape stays still as responses scroll independently inside each column;
headers stay visible. Narrow displays scroll all five columns and the landscape
together horizontally to preserve alignment. Responses retain their saved stage
and can be moved through drag and drop or the compact three-dot menu.
