Gradebook and formative assessment — 11 November 2026

Teacher workflow
Open Assessment > Gradebook for unit reporting. Choose Activity records for the activity-based Automatic / AI, Teacher or Both view. See docs/unit-reporting.txt for unit selections, AI suggestions and publication review.
Automatic evidence currently comes from server-marked quizzes, with first/latest/best attempts shown separately. Unit reporting now offers private AI grade suggestions from aligned evidence; the teacher reviews, accepts or modifies them and publishes separately. Published lesson ratings and Live Board judgments remain teacher evidence; they are not relabelled AI results.

Create an activity from a lesson, quiz, assignment, master notebook page, or manually for paper work and observations. Formative and summative purposes are separate. Formats: feedback only, points, percentage, letter grade, standards levels. School criterion judgments use Beginning / Approaching / Meeting / Exceeding, with Not enough evidence as an explicit state. They do not claim a validated numeric conversion to a framework's attainment scale.

Record the evidence type, observation, support, optional grade, targeted-standard judgments, feedback, next step, recheck date and a private note. Save draft & next student supports quick teacher recording. Exit-ticket and hinge-question evidence can be recorded here; this is not a new student polling or question-authoring system.

Only explicit teacher publication changes the result visible to students and linked parents. New drafts preserve the prior publication. Publication snapshots the activity details and, for quiz-linked records, captures trusted automatic evidence server-side. Automatic marks never overwrite the teacher result. Missing, absent, exempt and not observed remain distinct from zero. No cross-scale average or mastery conversion is calculated. Reassessment history is retained; private notes and audit details never enter family projections.

Activity title, date and success criteria can be corrected. Once evidence exists, scale, source and standards stay fixed; use a follow-up activity to change them. Historical publications retain their earlier details until explicitly republished.

Data and permissions
API: /api/gradebook/classes/{classId}[/{activityId}]. Session, current class membership, teacher role, origin and expected-account checks guard mutations. Family GET responses contain only linked learners' latest published snapshots. Archived/deleted classes and withdrawn class members fail closed. R2 records live under gradebook/{classId}/activities/{id}.json. Conditional ETag writes and activity revisions reject conflicting saves. Request IDs make retries idempotent. Each student/activity retains up to 200 historical observations; the server refuses further writes and asks for a follow-up assessment, rather than truncating history. An 8 MiB activity cap bounds records. The existing private R2 binding is reused; no D1 migration is required.

Research informing the design
NESA: formative evidence becomes useful when it guides teaching, feedback and opportunities to improve:
https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/assessment-and-reporting/formative-assessment
EEF: feedback principles and effective use matter more than choosing written versus verbal delivery:
https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/feedback
British Council: transparency and human control when using AI for marking and assessment:
https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/professional-development/teachers/using-digital-technologies/ai-guidelines-teachers
These design decisions are recommendations, not a claim of compliance with every school's grading policy.
